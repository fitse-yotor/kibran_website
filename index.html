✦ The Making ✦

From Forest
to Heirloom

A single cabinet takes up to six weeks. This is why.

፩

Wood Selection

Artisans travel to regional sawmills — Jimma, Gondar, Bale — selecting only first-growth cedar and rosewood. Each plank is checked by hand for grain, density, and freedom from faults.

፪

Seasoning

Timber is stacked and air-dried for a minimum of twelve months in our Addis Ababa workshop. No kiln shortcut — slow seasoning prevents future cracking and gives the wood its characteristic weight.

፫

Pattern Drawing

Master carvers transfer the design onto the prepared surface using charcoal and straight edge — no computers, no projectors. The geometry must be exact; errors in the underdrawing become permanent in the wood.

፬

Hand Carving

This is the heart of the work. Using V-gouges, chisels, and mallets unchanged in form since the craftsman's grandfather's time, each groove is cut by hand. The rope-twist alone on a standard cabinet takes two full days.

፭

Staining & Finishing

Natural Ethiopian black stain is applied in three coats, each hand-rubbed. The carved grooves catch the stain differently to the raised surfaces — this is what makes the gold-on-ebony contrast so striking. Final coat: beeswax and linseed oil.

፮

Delivery

Each piece leaves our workshop wrapped in hand-woven netela cloth. We deliver within Addis Ababa and ship internationally by specialist art freight. Every piece includes a signed certificate of craftsmanship.

✦ Our Heritage ✦

Born from the
Heart of Ethiopia

For over three decades, Kibran has transformed native Ethiopian timber into heirloom furniture. The workshop was founded in 1987 by master carver Kibran Tesfaye, who learned his craft from his own father in the ancient tradition of the Gondar church carvers.

No machines — only inherited craft, human hands, and tools passed down through generations. Today, twelve artisans carry the tradition forward, taking on commissions from private homes, embassies, luxury hotels, and museums across Ethiopia and internationally.

✦ ፩ · ፪ — Sacred Inspirations ✦

Drawn from
Ancient Ethiopia

The motifs carved into every Kibran piece echo two of Ethiopia's most sacred architectural legacies.

Lalibela · 12th c.

The Bet Giyorgis Cross

The tripled Greek cross carved into the roof of Lalibela's rock-hewn Church of St. George. Three nested crosses represent the Trinity — the same geometry appears in Kibran's inlay borders.

Axum · 3rd–4th c.

The Axum Stele & Star

The great obelisks of Axum bear window-like relief bands and are crowned with the Aksumite 8-pointed star — two overlapping squares symbolising heaven and earth united. This motif crowns every Kibran cabinet door.
